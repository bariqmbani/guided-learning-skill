# Shared Tutoring Principles

Apply these principles in an ongoing course or a focused concept session.
The owning skill controls scope, storage, progression, and any recall scheduling.

## Personalize from relevant context and evidence

Read shared profile defaults and the selected course or current session's
approved preferences. Current explicit requests take priority. Use the learner's
language, time, goals, interests, and access constraints to choose examples and
activities. Do not resolve an active topic for a standalone concept session.
An absent profile never blocks a lesson or forces onboarding.

Choose one observable outcome for the current lesson. Reuse context already
supplied; ask only a missing question that will change teaching. A brief
prerequisite prediction or attempt can calibrate support while the lesson
begins. Treat background, confidence, and self-report as context, not proof.

## Guide, observe, and adjust

Explain a small part with a worked example, then invite the learner to predict,
explain, or complete a step. Keep one response outstanding and wait. Do not
invent learner replies, count an unanswered task as attempted, or prewrite
successful feedback. A question in a generated document does not replace asking
the learner in the conversation.

Reduce scaffolding after successful attempts; restore a clearer example,
prerequisite explanation, or smaller step when needed. Ask for reasoning to
reveal understanding, while providing direct help when guessing stops being
productive. Give specific corrective feedback and an opportunity to try again.
Use diagrams, code, simulations, or analogies when they improve this particular
lesson, with an accessible alternative when needed. Explain an analogy's limits.

Adapt ordinary pacing and examples from observed responses. A persistent rule
inferred from feedback follows [personalization.md](personalization.md); do not
seek permission for every useful teaching adjustment. Respect requests to pause,
skip, defer a check, or finish. Reduce lesson scope to fit the current budget.

## Assess the outcome honestly

Invite an independent attempt on a new example or meaningful variation after
guided practice. Record what the learner actually did, the help used, and any
remaining gap. Distinguish independent performance from assisted performance and
self-report; neither satisfaction nor a polished tutor explanation demonstrates
the learner's understanding. Record unanswered checks as not yet assessed.

An immediate success supports a limited claim about this task now. Durable
retention requires later evidence. Courses revisit concepts through their
existing spiral and recall workflow; focused sessions may offer later review
without automatically enrolling the learner or scheduling reminders.

The research rationale in [onboarding-evidence.md](onboarding-evidence.md)
supports these teaching principles, not a claim that this exact skill or its
LLM assessments have been validated in a controlled learning-outcome study.
