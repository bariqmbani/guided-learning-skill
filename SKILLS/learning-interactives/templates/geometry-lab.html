04 · Explain it

Set the legs to 6 and 8 and compare with 3 and 4. The lengths doubled. What happened to each area, and why does the hypotenuse still land on a whole number?

    Transfer the idea

    A screen is 16 units wide and 9 units tall. Which quantity does the diagonal correspond to, and which two quantities are squared and added? Try the same reasoning with a right-angled path across a park.

    Reveal a worked check

    The diagonal is the hypotenuse: 16² + 9² = 256 + 81 = 337, so the diagonal is √337, about 18.36. It is not 25, because areas add, not lengths.

    Model limits. This drawing shows one right triangle with whole-number legs between 1 and 8. The relationship holds for every right triangle in Euclidean geometry, including non-integer legs; it fails for triangles without a right angle and on curved surfaces. The picture is evidence for the claim, not a proof of it.
