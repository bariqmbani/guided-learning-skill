02 · Build the solution

Move each step until the list reads as a solution you could hand in. Use the arrows, or drag if you prefer.

    The worked solution

    Dividing first removes the whole bracket in one move, because the bracket is multiplied by 3 as a single quantity. Expanding first also works and takes one extra step.

    Why this order?

    Every legal step keeps both sides equal. The useful question is not "what is allowed?" but "what gets x alone fastest?"

      03 · Explain it

      Subtracting 4 first is legal but unhelpful. Write what the equation becomes if you do it, and say why that leaves more work than dividing first.

      Reveal a worked check

      Subtracting 4 from both sides of 3(x + 4) = 27 gives 3(x + 4) − 4 = 23, which is still a bracket times 3 minus 4. Nothing was undone, because the 4 inside the bracket is not a term on its own: it is multiplied by 3 along with x.

      Transfer the idea

      Order the steps for 5(y − 2) = 35 without rebuilding the list. Which step changes, and which stays identical? Then try 3(x + 4) = 28 and say why the answer is no longer a whole number.

      Model limits. One accepted order is checked here. Expanding the bracket first reaches the same answer by a longer route and is also correct; this activity rewards the shorter route and asks you to justify it. Equations with the unknown on both sides need a step this list does not contain.
      An order you can justify beats an order you remember.
