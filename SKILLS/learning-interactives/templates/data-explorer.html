04 · Explain it

Set the slider so that both treatments receive the same share of severe cases. Which treatment leads overall now, and what does that tell you about the pooled comparison you started with?

Reveal a worked check

With equal shares the pooled winner matches the within-group winner, because the groups no longer differ in difficulty. The original pooled result was driven by who received each treatment, not by how well either treatment worked.

Transfer the idea

A department reports a higher pass rate than another, and each of its courses has a lower pass rate. What would you need to know about enrolment before quoting either number? Name the grouping variable you would ask for.

Data provenance and limits. These 700 records are illustrative and fabricated for this lesson; they are not from a study and must not be quoted as evidence about any treatment. The success rate inside each severity is fixed at 93% and 73% for A and 87% and 69% for B; the slider only changes how many cases of each severity a treatment receives. The pattern they show, a reversal when a grouping variable is ignored, is real and is known as Simpson's paradox. Within-group rates stay fixed as you move the slider, which is a teaching device, not how a real trial behaves. Nothing here supports a causal claim: that needs a design that controls how cases are assigned.
Ask who is in the group before you compare the rates.
