    A useful distinction

    Under an independent, fixed-probability model, larger samples tend to have less relative variation. A particular sequence can still move away from the expected proportion for a while. The next toss does not compensate for earlier outcomes.

    Transfer the idea

    Two surveys sample 10 and 100 people from the same population. Which result would you expect to vary more across repeated samples? What sampling biases could make both misleading?

    Model limits. This uses a seeded pseudorandom generator to illustrate independent coin tosses with a fixed probability. It is reproducible, not a source of physical randomness. A run of at most 200 tosses does not prove a long-run law, establish a coin's fairness, or include the selection biases found in real surveys.
    A sample is evidence with variability, not a promise about the next outcome.
